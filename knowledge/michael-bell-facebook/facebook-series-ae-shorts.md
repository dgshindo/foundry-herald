# Facebook Series — Streamlining After Effects for Video Shorts

## Series Purpose
Document how Michael Bell began streamlining repetitive parts of creating video Shorts in Adobe After Effects and gradually turned that workflow into a larger production tool.

The series should feel like Michael telling friends how the project evolved, not like an After Effects tutorial course or a product advertisement.

## Part 1 of 7 — The Problem Wasn't After Effects. It Was Repetition.
Creating a Short repeatedly required the same setup work: compositions, audio, subtitles, branding, effects, end screen, and render setup. The creative decisions were not the main problem. Rebuilding the machinery every time was.

## Part 2 of 7 — If I Do It Twice, I Start Wondering Why I'm Doing It Manually
The first useful automation was creating predictable project structure and standard compositions automatically.

## Part 3 of 7 — Subtitles Shouldn't Be a Production Project
Subtitle/lyric workflows consume surprising production time. The Forge began handling SRT import, subtitle placement, styling, timing-related workflow, and reusable treatment.

## Part 4 of 7 — Branding Should Be Data, Not a Checklist
Instead of manually remembering fonts, colors, sigils, text treatments, and end-screen details, define a brand profile and let the production tool apply the known rules.

## Part 5 of 7 — Build the Atmosphere Once. Reuse the System.
Smoke, embers, sigil treatments, animation behaviors, and other atmospheric elements became reusable components.

## Part 6 of 7 — The Moment a Script Became an Application
At some point the project stopped being a collection of small automation scripts and gained project creation, configuration, brand profiles, imports, reusable effects, save/open behavior, end screens, and rendering controls.

## Part 7 of 7 — Automation Isn't About Removing the Creative Work
The purpose is not to make every Short automatically. It is to automate repetitive production mechanics so more time can go into what the video should look, sound, and feel like.

## Series Rules
Focus on one part at a time. Do not recap the whole project every time. Keep each post useful independently. Avoid repeating the same opening joke or scope-creep punchline. Use only established Forge capabilities. Do not imply unfinished features are complete. Keep the tone personal and conversational.
